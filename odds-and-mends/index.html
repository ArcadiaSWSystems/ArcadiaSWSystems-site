Odds & Mends

You know those mobile game ads that make everyone want to play, for a game that never turns out to exist? This one does. A cozy repair-shop mystery for iPhone: mend what the harbor brings in, pay the rent, and uncover the truth hidden inside ten repairs.

In beta · Coming soon to iPhone Get notified at launch

Gameplay

What you see is what you play.

No trailer, no mockups, no pre-rendered scenes. These clips were recorded straight from the game at real speed. Every repair happens at your bench, under your finger.

Screens from the game

A shop, a town, and a secret.

Ilsa Lumen mended things for the whole of Kessa Harbor until the night the old shipyard burned. Now her stall is yours: her bench, her tools, her notebook, and the last page in it, ten repairs and the words "kept safe inside".

What's inside

Cozy on the surface.
Choices all the way down.

Hands-on repairs Clean, unscrew, solder, stitch, glue, rewire, polish and tune: fifteen repairs at the bench, and about fifty things to mend, from a harbor lantern to a moon-lute.
Run the shop Price every ticket, choose genuine or cheap parts, and keep up with the rent that comes due every tenth day.
A quiet mystery Each month one of Ilsa's repairs comes back across your counter, and its owner doesn't know what's inside.
Decisions that count Call the Watch on a thief or keep quiet. Who you trust with what you find decides how it ends.
100 days, 12 endings Ten months, the harbor's news different every run, and an epilogue for everyone you met. Play again from any month.
Every line spoken The cast, the townsfolk and you, all voiced. Voices off and larger text are one tap away.
No timers, no ads Play at your own pace. A day takes a few minutes, which makes it a good fit for a coffee break.
Stays on your phone Your progress and settings live on your device. No accounts, no analytics, no tracking.
🏮

It started with an ad.

You've seen them: gorgeous little repair-shop games in mobile ads that turn out to be something else entirely. We wanted to know if the game they promise could be made for real. It could. Then we made it our own, with its own town, its own story and its own mystery.
